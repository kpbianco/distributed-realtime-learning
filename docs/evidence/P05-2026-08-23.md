## Governing numerical contract

For paired one-way timestamps, receiver-minus-sender clock offset `theta`, and true network delay
`d_i`, the observable is:

`z_i = receiver timestamp_i - sender timestamp_i = theta + d_i`.

P05 provides an external anchor only for teaching: `d_min` must be the exact attainable total
fixed-delay floor, and at least one paired sample must attain it. Under those conditions,
`theta_hat = min(z)-d_min` and `d_hat_i = z_i-theta_hat`. One-way observations alone do not make
the decomposition unique.

The exact baseline uses eight samples, a `7 ms` offset, a `3 ms` attainable floor, and queue shape
`[0 0.25 0.75 0.5 1 0.25 0.5 0]` with `8 ms` peak:

- variable queue delay: `[0 2 6 4 8 2 4 0] ms`;
- true network delay: `[3 5 9 7 11 5 7 3] ms`;
- observed timestamp difference: `[10 12 16 14 18 12 14 10] ms`;
- anchored offset estimate: `7 ms`; and
- estimated network delay: `[3 5 9 7 11 5 7 3] ms`, with zero reference residual.

The offset sweep `[-8 0 12] ms` produces observed minima `[-5 3 15] ms`, exact anchored offset
estimates, fixed `8 ms` spread, and invariant estimated network delay. The queue-peak sweep
`[0 4 12] ms` produces fixed `7 ms` offset estimates, spreads `[0 4 12] ms`, and maximum true
delays `[3 7 15] ms`.

The deliberately broken case `model(8,7,3,8,5,3)` hides `5 ms` of common network delay. It has
true delays `[8 10 14 12 16 10 12 8] ms` and observations
`[15 17 21 19 23 17 19 15] ms`. Those observations are exactly identical to
`model(8,12,3,8,0,3)`. The false anchor estimates `12 ms` offset, overstates offset by `5 ms`,
understates every delay by `5 ms`, and still reconstructs every observation exactly. The anchor
status is exposed only as simulator ground truth, never as endpoint-observable evidence.
